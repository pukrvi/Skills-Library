# Fundamentals and Targeting

## What great means on LinkedIn
A great LinkedIn post does three things at once.
- Stops the scroll with a hook.
- Earns attention with value density and structure.
- Creates one clear action such as comment follow DM click or reply.

## Choose one primary action
Pick one primary action before you write.
- Engagement. Comments and conversation.
- Growth. Follows and profile visits.
- Leads. DMs and opt ins.
- Authority. Shares and saves.

## Non negotiables
- A single clear promise in the first two lines.
- Proof you earned the lesson such as numbers story screenshots or what you tried.
- One main idea not a kitchen sink.
- Skimmable mobile first formatting.
- A call to action that matches the value.

## The simplest definition of value
Value equals clarity. Provide a framework checklist script or steps that someone can use today.

## Pick the right post type
Choose the archetype that matches your evidence.
- Data driven. Use when you have numbers. Focus on the lesson and what changed.
- Contrarian. Use when you can challenge a common belief. Show reasoning and examples.
- Story. Use when you changed your mind or behavior. Keep story to about twenty percent.
- Tactical. Use when you can teach steps or checklists. Make it specific and include scripts.
- Operator angle. Use when you can show a repeatable internal process.

Fallback rule.
- Numbers means data driven.
- Strong belief means contrarian.
- Transformation means story.
- Checklist means tactical.
- Internal systems means operator angle.

## Audience targeting that works
Pick one primary reader and write like you are DMing them.
Define.
- Role.
- Context.
- Pain.
- Desire.

Use the so what test for each paragraph.
- So what.
- What do I want them to do with this.
- What would they screenshot.

If the audience feels too broad narrow by tool use case stage or outcome.
