# Hooks and Structures

## Hook engineering
Your hook is a contract not a headline.

Five hook styles.
- Data. A specific metric or outcome.
- Contrarian. A belief challenge with a reason.
- Story. A moment that changed your thinking.
- Tactical. A promise of steps or a checklist.
- Role based. A claim aimed at a specific job role.

Hook rules.
- Use specifics such as numbers tools timeframe outcome.
- Avoid throat clearing.
- Create curiosity without being vague.

Hook upgrade checklist.
- Can someone repeat it from memory.
- Does it imply a clear lesson.
- Does it attract the right reader and repel the wrong one.

Fallback hook starters.
- I used to think X until Y.
- Most people do X here is why it fails.
- I tracked X for Y days. Surprising result Z.
- If you want X stop doing Y.

## The first six lines
The first six lines should.
- Establish credibility.
- Name the problem.
- Promise the payoff.
- Preview the structure.

Formatting rules for mobile.
- One idea per line.
- Zero to two sentences per paragraph.
- Use bullets where possible.
- Keep lines short.

## The giveaway backbone structure
Use this structure to keep momentum.
1. Opening line with the promise.
2. Past tense list for relatability.
3. Problem explanation that makes the cost clear.
4. Solution as a framework not random tips.
5. Knowledge and instructions with templates.
6. Highlight value with benefits and outcomes.
7. Call to action that drives the primary action.

Even without a giveaway you can keep the structure and change the call to action.

## Past tense lists
Goal. Create a mirror moment.
How. Start with I used to or Most people and list four to eight mistakes.
Rules.
- Keep it short.
- Do not teach yet.
- Do not defend yourself.
- Do not add paragraphs.

## Problem explanation
Goal. Turn yes into oh no.
Use cause to effect.
Bundle into three buckets when possible.
- Clarity problem.
- Control problem.
- Craft problem.

Fallback. Use a simple because chain and keep it calm.

## Solution section
Teach a system.
Format options.
- Pillars. Three to five memorable categories.
- Steps. A numbered sequence.

If you have too many tips group them into three to five categories.
