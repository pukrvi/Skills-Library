# Research and Editing

## Research standards
Your credibility comes from the quality of your sources and the transparency of your method.

Prioritize sources in this order:
1. Primary sources: vendor docs product pages pricing pages security pages release notes.
2. First party proof: your testing notes screenshots benchmarks customer interviews.
3. Independent sources: analyst reports reputable media academic papers conference talks.
4. Aggregators: use cautiously and cross check.

## Source log requirement
Maintain a source log for every factual claim.
For each source capture:
- Claim supported.
- Source title.
- URL.
- Publisher.
- Date accessed.
- Any important caveats.

## Fact checking rules
- If you cannot verify a number do not publish it as a number.
- Separate observations from conclusions.
- When evidence is thin use language like suggests indicates or may and explain why.

## Adding data stats and examples
Aim for one proof element every 150 to 250 words.
Proof element types:
- Data point: market size growth rate adoption metrics.
- Benchmark: time saved conversion lift productivity change.
- Example: a short real world workflow.
- Mini case study: before after outcome with constraints.

Mini case study template:
- Context: who and what environment.
- Problem: what was failing.
- Intervention: what they changed using the platform.
- Outcome: measurable result and timeframe.
- Lesson: what a reader should copy.

## Tables charts and visuals
- Use tables for comparisons and criteria.
- Use simple charts when they clarify magnitude or trends.
- Always label what the data represents and where it came from.

## Drafting workflow
1. Outline first with headings that match intent.
2. Write in passes:
   - Pass 1: complete draft fast.
   - Pass 2: add proof blocks and examples.
   - Pass 3: tighten language and improve scannability.
3. Add FAQs and glossary last.

## Editing checklist
- Clarity: each paragraph has one point.
- Specificity: replace vague words with concrete details.
- Skim value: short paragraphs bullets and tables.
- Accuracy: every fact has a source log entry.
- Balance: include limitations and tradeoffs.
- Freshness: confirm pricing and feature claims are current at time of writing.
- CTA: include the next step such as request a demo download checklist or compare platforms.

## Final publish checklist
- Title and meta description match the promise.
- Table of contents works on mobile.
- Internal links added where helpful.
- All outbound links open in a new tab if your CMS supports it.
- Last updated line included for buyer guides.
