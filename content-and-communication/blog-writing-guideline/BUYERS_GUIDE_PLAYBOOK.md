# Buyers Guide Playbook

## Core promise of a buyers guide
A buyers guide helps a reader choose. It should reduce uncertainty and shorten evaluation time.

## Recommended structure for a 2025 buyers guide
1. Introduction and who this guide is for
2. What the category is and why it matters now
3. Use cases and outcomes
4. Buying criteria and evaluation framework
5. Must have features and nice to have features
6. Comparison table of top platforms
7. Deep dive reviews of each platform
8. Pricing and total cost considerations
9. Security privacy and compliance considerations
10. Implementation plan and rollout checklist
11. RFP questions and scoring rubric
12. Recommendations by company size and use case
13. FAQs
14. Glossary

## Buying criteria module
Use a consistent set of criteria so comparisons feel fair.
Suggested criteria for AI role play platforms:
- Scenario quality: realism coverage and update cadence.
- Feedback and coaching: scoring rubric coaching prompts and actionable insights.
- Modalities: text voice video multi turn conversations.
- Personalization: persona setup product context and industry tailoring.
- Analytics: skill gaps trends cohort reporting and manager views.
- Integrations: CRM LMS SSO HRIS content libraries.
- Governance: admin controls audit logs data retention and permissions.
- Content authoring: scenario builder branching logic and approval workflow.
- Evaluation reliability: consistency across runs and calibration options.
- Time to value: onboarding effort and template library.

## Platform shortlisting table
Create one table early so readers can compare quickly.

Suggested columns:
- Platform
- Best for
- Key strengths
- Key limitations
- Pricing model
- Integrations
- AI modality
- Notes

## Platform review template
Use the same mini template for every platform.
- Overview: what it is in one sentence.
- Best for: the most fitting buyer profile.
- Standout features: 3 to 5 bullets.
- Workflow: how a user uses it step by step.
- Integrations: key systems it connects to.
- Pricing: what is known and how it is packaged.
- Pros and cons: balanced bullets.
- What to ask in a demo: 3 to 5 questions.

## Pricing and cost module
Cover both direct and indirect cost.
- Subscription or seat cost.
- Usage or credits.
- Implementation services.
- Content creation time.
- Admin overhead.

## Security and compliance module
For sales training and employee data this is often a deciding factor.
Include:
- SSO and role based access.
- Data retention and deletion.
- Training data policy for AI models.
- Audit logs.
- Compliance claims and where to verify them.

## Recommendations section
Provide clear recommendation paths.
Examples:
- Best for startups.
- Best for mid market enablement teams.
- Best for regulated enterprise.
- Best for multilingual global teams.
- Best for voice role play.

## Scoring rubric
If you score platforms keep it simple and explain the method.
- Use 5 to 7 criteria.
- Weight the top 2 criteria for your audience.
- Explain what a 5 means for each criterion.

## What makes a buyers guide rank
- Clear definitions.
- Transparent selection criteria.
- Comparison table.
- Up to date notes with last updated date.
- Real examples and proof blocks.
