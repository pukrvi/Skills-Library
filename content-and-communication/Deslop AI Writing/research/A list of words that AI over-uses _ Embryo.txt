							Below is a simple list of words that AI overuses. I have scoured the web to find various lists of words that are overused by various AIs from Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, Qwen, Deepseek and several others. I then spent an awful lot of time cross-referencing them. And upon building the list, I used the excellent GPTZero AI Vocabulary checker – which is highly recommended – to show results (or not) when overused AI words were used.
After all of this, whenever I asked various AI chat bots to NOT use the list of words and phrases below, the content produced by each chatbot (especially Gemini) became much better for it. In my experience, ChatGPT (various versions) was not the best at this, surprisingly. Deepseek was OK, but Gemini was surprisingly the best at writing content while avoiding such words.
Does removing these ‘dead words’ and phrases mean that AI content is then undetectable?
No. Absolutely not. AI content gets detected in many more ways than just a list of overused words/phrases.
By omitting these words (I typically request this for any content I ask for by uploading a text file – some AIs are better than others at sticking to this, even if you tell them 100 times), all content written using AI was still deemed as somewhere between 90-100% ‘AI written’ when checked with GPTZero.
But interestingly, after leaving out these words from content output, manually converting the AI-generated content into content flagged as ‘written by a human’ took much less time. It probably took less than a third of the time to change AI content into human content when the list of words and phrases below was not used in the content. That’s some kind of progress I think in the battle against detection of AI content.
I do think that trying to convince Google and others that AI content is human-written is a losing battle, and won’t spend much more time on it, other than these few days of experimentation. But it was fun while it lasted.
What could this experiment help you with when writing content?
Well, let’s say you were asked to write a few hundred words about how SEO has changed over the years for a page on a website. By first asking your preferred AI to avoid any mentions of the words and phrases below, and then asking it to give you a history of how SEO has changed, you will almost certainly be given a piece of content that has lots of research embedded in it that you would have had to spend a few hours or more to find. Again, just for reference, I have found Gemini 2.0 Flash to be the best for this at the time of writing, but you do you.
And because you will be returned a somewhat structured piece of content, with no ‘poison’ or ‘dead’ words, you can then – rather quickly – make something that would be 90%+ detected as AI content into something that would be 90%+ detected as human content. An extra tip is if you used a ‘web search’ option when you make a query, which will mean your AI will also check the web for data, which should lead to better information (if your preferred AI has this option).
I would say that this method of working would be good for any content that is under 500 words. Things such as product descriptions or city pages of a website are good examples. But I would strongly suggest that you use a quality AI detector like the aforementioned GPTZero (I have no affiliation, it’s just good software).
And finally, the AI word list
Now, a bit of a disclaimer. I don’t advise in any way, shape, or form, that you write using AI for your websites. By all means, use AI to help write internal documents, or indeed anything in which your content is not used online. As long as it reads well, of course.
