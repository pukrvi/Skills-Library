# World Building for Persuasion

## Why it matters
New ideas feel fake until the reader can imagine them operating in reality.

## Root with concrete examples
Rules
- Prefer named examples over abstract claims.
- Show what happens, who does it, and what changes.

Example patterns
- Case: Person or company does X, gets Y, avoids Z.
- Contrast: Before vs after.
- Mechanism: Step by step how it works.

## Micro interactions add depth
Add small operational details that imply consistency:
- Tools, checklists, artifacts, meetings, or routines.
- Specific constraints and tradeoffs.
- Tiny behaviors that a real practitioner would mention.

## Create productive dissonance
Hold two truths that conflict, then resolve:
- You say you value A, but you spend most time on B.
- You want speed, but your process rewards caution.

Resolution pattern
1. Name both truths without judgment.
2. Show why the conflict persists.
3. Offer your idea as the resolution that preserves the reader’s values.

## Checklist
- Do I have at least one named example.
- Do I include one micro detail that only an insider would mention.
- Do I create and resolve one tension the reader recognizes.
