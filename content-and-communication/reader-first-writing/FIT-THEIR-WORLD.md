# Fit Into Their World

## The red pill rule
Before you lead the reader to a new idea, prove you belong in their existing world.

## Atomic agreements
Atomic agreements are statements that:
- Are already true in the reader’s worldview.
- Require near zero effort to accept.
- Create momentum and nodding.

How to generate them:
1. List the reader’s goals and constraints.
2. Write 5 to 10 true statements they would endorse.
3. Pick 3 to 7 that are most universal and least controversial.
4. Place 1 to 2 at the top of the piece.

Examples
- We all want the company to grow.
- Everyone is fighting for attention now.
- Hiring is expensive and slow.
- Customers trust what they can see.

## Signal in group status
Use the tribe’s shibboleths:
- Preferred jargon, metrics, and metaphors.
- Names of tools, roles, and workflows they use.
- The problems they complain about in public.

Rules
- Use lingo only when you can use it correctly.
- Do not sprinkle buzzwords as decoration.
- Mirror the reader’s register, not your own.

## The frame shift
A reliable sequence:
1. Their world: the premise they already hold.
2. Shared problem: the friction inside that premise.
3. Your idea: a solution that preserves their values while changing the method.

Template
- If you believe [THEIR PREMISE], then you already care about [VALUE].
- The problem is [COST OR TENSION] is rising.
- Which means the most aligned move is [YOUR IDEA], because it buys [BENEFIT] without sacrificing [VALUE].

## Where to place the pivot
- After 2 to 4 sentences of agreement and belonging.
- After you name a shared pain in their language.
- Before you start explaining your solution.
