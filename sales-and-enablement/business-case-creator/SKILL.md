# Business Case Creator

Use this skill to turn discovery evidence into a business case a buyer champion can forward internally. The output should help a real decision group understand why change matters, what options exist, why the recommended path wins, what success looks like, and what must be validated next.

## Start Here

Read `references/workflow.md` before building a full case. Use the supporting references when the user asks for that output:

- `references/executive-memo-template.md` for executive memos, champion emails, one-pagers, deck outlines, and full reports.
- `references/scorecard.md` for case quality scoring and gap plans.
- `references/discovery-gap-questions.md` for validation questions and next discovery prompts.

## Inputs

Work with any combination of:

- Discovery call transcripts, meeting notes, emails, CRM notes, or account research.
- Stakeholders, roles, decision process, timeline, current workflow, current tools, and constraints.
- Product or solution context, pricing, implementation assumptions, and customer outcomes.
- Metrics such as team size, usage, volume, frequency, cycle time, conversion rates, cost, revenue, retention, or risk.

If important inputs are missing, proceed with clear assumptions instead of blocking. Ask targeted follow-up questions only when the missing information would materially change the recommendation.

## Evidence Rules

Every meaningful claim must be labeled:

- `VERIFIED`: directly supported by source material. Include a short quote, timestamp, or source note when available.
- `ASSUMPTION`: inferred from available evidence. Explain the inference and how to validate it.
- `FALLBACK`: based on a neutral benchmark or default because no account-specific evidence exists. State that it is directional.

Never invent quotes, people, numbers, products, or training assets. Do not present inferred numbers as facts. If you use a formula, show the inputs and label each input as verified, assumed, or fallback.

## Default Workflow

For a full business case, complete these steps in order:

1. Normalize the deal context.
2. Extract current state, pain, impact, stakeholders, and urgency.
3. Map decision criteria, options, and risks.
4. Quantify value and cost of inaction.
5. Generate the buyer-ready business case.
6. Coach the internal champion.
7. Produce an assumption and validation log.
8. Score the case and identify gaps.

## Output Modes

Default to a full champion pack unless the user asks for a narrower artifact.

- `Quick email`: short forwardable note for the champion.
- `One-pager`: concise business case with problem, impact, recommendation, proof, and next steps.
- `Executive memo`: structured decision memo using the Five Agreements.
- `Deck outline`: slide-by-slide executive narrative.
- `Full report`: complete case, scorecard, validation log, and coaching notes.
- `Scorecard only`: quality score, gaps, and next best discovery questions.
- `Value model only`: formulas, assumptions, ranges, and validation plan.

## Quality Bar

A strong business case is specific to the buyer, forwardable by the champion, financially grounded, honest about assumptions, and clear about the next decision. Before responding, check that the final output includes:

- A crisp case for change.
- The current-state pain and business impact.
- A comparison of status quo, internal build, existing tools, and recommended path when relevant.
- Quantified value or a transparent explanation of why quantification is not yet defensible.
- Stakeholder-specific talking points.
- Risks, objections, and responses.
- A validation log for every assumption that could weaken the case.
