# Style and Tone

## Investor research voice
- Analytical and well argued.
- Accessible to investors operators and founders.
- Confident but not hypey.
- Clear opinions supported by evidence.

## Structure and readability
- Short paragraphs. 1 to 3 sentences.
- Frequent subheaders inside each main section.
- Use lists for frameworks and comparisons.
- Define jargon on first use.

## Claim discipline
- Separate facts from interpretation.
- When data is scarce. make reasoned inferences and explain the logic.
- Avoid absolute statements unless strongly supported.

## Editing passes
1. Clarity pass. cut filler and tighten sentences.
2. Logic pass. every section should support the thesis.
3. Specificity pass. replace vague words with numbers or concrete examples.
4. Skim pass. add subheaders and convert long paragraphs into bullets.
5. Balance pass. opportunities and risks should be similarly detailed.

## Common pitfalls
- Kitchen sink memos that cover everything but say nothing.
- Overreliance on buzzwords such as disruption or revolutionary.
- Long product descriptions that do not connect to buyer value.
- Competitive sections that list names without differentiation.

## Word count targeting
- Default 1000 to 2000 words.
- Allocate roughly.
  - Thesis and summary 15 percent.
  - Product and market 35 percent.
  - Competition and business model 25 percent.
  - Traction and valuation 15 percent.
  - Opportunities and risks 10 percent.
