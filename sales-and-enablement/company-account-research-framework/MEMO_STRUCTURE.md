# Memo Structure

Use this structure to produce a investor research style company memo. Keep sections short and skimmable with punchy subheaders.

## 1. Thesis
- 1 to 2 paragraphs framing the company and why it matters.
- Your central claim about trajectory, strategic importance, and potential.
- State the essence of the opportunity in plain terms.
- Preview what the reader should believe by the end.

## 2. Founding Story
- When and where the company started and by whom.
- Founder backgrounds and formative experiences tied to the problem.
- Original hypothesis and early product wedge.
- Early inflection points such as pivots, early customers, or platform shifts.
- Include narrative hooks that explain the company DNA.

## 3. Product
- What [Product Name] is today and who uses it.
- What makes it unique. Technical differentiation and workflow fit.
- Integrations and ecosystem positioning.
- Evolution from MVP to current state.

## 4. AI Solutions (if relevant)
- Where AI is used in [Product Name] and what it automates or improves.
- Proprietary versus third party models and data moats if any.
- How AI changes unit economics, scalability, or user experience.
- Key risks such as commoditization, dependency, and trust.

## 5. Market
- Define the market and the buyer and user personas.
- Estimate TAM, SAM, SOM when possible and show assumptions.
- Tailwinds and adoption dynamics.
- Geographic or regulatory dynamics.

## 6. Competition
- Direct competitors and adjacent players.
- A simple differentiation matrix and defensibility assessment.
- Barriers to entry and switching costs.
- How competition shapes strategy and positioning.

## 7. Business Model
- Revenue model and how pricing is structured.
- Packaging strategy and how expansion happens.
- Unit economics if available. Gross margins and CAC signals.
- Long term monetization opportunities.

## 8. Traction
- Headline metrics such as ARR, customers, users, GMV, retention.
- Funding history and notable investors.
- Expansion into new verticals or geographies.
- Partnerships and product ecosystem signals.

## 9. Valuation
- Latest round and valuation when available.
- Peer multiples and public comps when applicable.
- Valuation history and implied expectations.

## 10. Key Opportunities
- 3 to 6 growth vectors that are concrete and plausible.
- Product expansion, new segments, platform plays, partnerships, M and A.
- Tie each opportunity to a capability or advantage the company has.

## 11. Key Risks
- 3 to 6 risks that map to execution, market, competition, technology, and strategy.
- Write risks as falsifiable statements with leading indicators to watch.

## 12. Summary
- Restate the thesis with balanced synthesis.
- One liner takeaway on why this company matters and what the next 2 to 3 years could look like.
